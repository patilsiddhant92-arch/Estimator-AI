AME PRACTICE — THE ESTIMATORS' OWN RULES (addendum for use inside the estimating tool)
These rules come from AME's estimators. They are read with the rest of
these instructions; where they are more specific, they govern.
Add a rule as plain text below, one rule per paragraph.

FACTORY CONTROLS — AME PRACTICE: the controller and every end device on a factory/OEM-controls unit belong to the unit manufacturer unless the documents (sequence, schedule note, plan note, detail) specifically assign one to the BMS/ATC contractor. Write out EVERY manufacturer-furnished device the job names that the controls contractor installs or wires, as its own line: 'Install and Wire <device> (Furnished by Unit Mfr.)' or 'Wire <device> (Furnished by Unit Mfr.)'. A device the documents assign to the controls contractor on a factory unit gets the normal verbs (Furnish and Install, Furnish and Wire …) — explicit assignment always wins.

SPLIT SYSTEMS AND VRF — AME PRACTICE: write 'Low Voltage Interlock Wiring between Indoor and Outdoor Unit' when the units are field-connected; when the documents show refrigerant leak detection with shut-off valves or an alarm chime, write the wiring lines for them unless another party is named.

SHIPPED-LOOSE DEVICES — AME PRACTICE (stated by the estimator 2026-10-03): a device the unit manufacturer furnishes and ships loose is written 'Install and Wire <device> (Furnished by Unit Mfr.)' almost every time. 'Wire <device> (Furnished by Unit Mfr.)' is only for a device the manufacturer already mounts. When unsure whether it is mounted, write Install and Wire.

DAMPER ACTUATORS — AME PRACTICE (stated 2026-10-03): a damper actuator is written under the system whose fan or unit it serves (the exhaust fan's damper, the make-up air fan's damper, the unit's outside air damper), in that system's block, not gathered into one Motorized Damper block at the end. A standalone damper block is only for dampers no fan or unit claims (stair-top vent dampers interlocked with a smoke detector, generator room dampers). Fire/smoke dampers keep their own block: only the interlock wiring and end-switch status, as the Master Template's Fire / Smoke Dampers entry writes them.

VRF SYSTEMS — AME PRACTICE (stated 2026-10-03): write the VRF the way the Master Template's VRF System entry does: one line per indoor unit for 'Install and Wire Programmable Thermostat (Furnished by Unit Mfr.)', the remote space sensor, leak sensor or local RAM module only when the documents call for them, 'VRF Communication Wiring' once, the condensate pump float switch tie-in when there are condensate pumps, then the central controller / BMS communication wiring, Map Available Points and the Mfr. Rep line once for the system. Do not turn every item in the sequence's "BMS contractor shall wire…" list into its own 'Wire …' line; the interlock wiring between indoor units, branch controllers and condensers is the manufacturer's VRF communication wiring unless the documents say otherwise.

ELECTRIC DUCT HEATERS — AME PRACTICE (stated 2026-10-03): when the duct heater serves a unit with packaged/factory controls (an ERV, a packaged RTU, an Enervex make-up fan), AME's scope is the interlock only: wire the heater's enable/interlock and auxiliary contact to the unit's controls; the heater's own SCR control, airflow switch and cut-outs come with the heater. When the duct heater is controlled by an AME DDC controller (a field-DDC fan or unit), AME writes the full duct heater scope: discharge air temperature sensor, SCR control signal, status/alarm, enable, as the heater entry of the Master Template gives it.

PACKAGED UNITS — WHAT AME DOES (stated by the estimator 2026-10-03): a packaged unit comes with its factory controller, its end devices and its equipment wired inside at the factory. Everything inside the unit that the sequence describes — valve control, fan control, DX coil staging, leak control, reheat — is already wired in the factory and is NOT written as scope lines. AME's work on a packaged unit is: (1) 'Install and Wire <device> (Furnished by Unit Mfr.)' for the devices the factory cannot mount and ships loose — the space sensor or thermostat, the supply air temperature sensor, the supply static pressure sensor and the like — wired to the OEM controller; (2) any device the sequence explicitly asks the controls contractor to PROVIDE, written with the normal Furnish and Install verb; (3) the three integration lines — 'Furnish BMS Communication Wiring to Factory Controls', 'Map Available Points Via Mfr. Provided BMS Interface', and the Mfr. Rep line — which cover every 'monitor … at the BMS' sentence in the sequence. Do not write a line per monitored point or per internal device by owner. The Master Template is how the system looks in the ideal case; when the sequence says something explicit, take it into account — AME's responsibility is the correct Furnish / Install / Wire ownership on each line, not restating the sequence.

CONTRADICTED COUNTS — AME PRACTICE (stated 2026-10-03): when the documents give two counts, the higher count prints, flagged, with both counts named beside it. The estimator decides on review.

DAMPER ACTUATORS — WHERE THEY GO (stated 2026-10-03): the damper-to-fan relationship is on the plans and the risers. When the duct can be traced on the plan or through the riser floors, the actuator goes under that fan's or unit's block. When it cannot be traced, the dampers go in their own block with the sheet reference and the total count, flagged for the estimator.
